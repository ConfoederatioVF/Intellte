# Methodology

This gazetteer compiles the geographical coordinates, historical and indigenous names, occupation spans, and benchmark population histories of 193 individual settlements across Western Siberia (the Ob-Irtysh river basin, ~2,4M km²) from 6000BC to 1900AD [@Chernykh2022]. Reading directly from the regional settlement dataset in the standard Stadester format, the compilation covers prehistoric, Bronze Age, and Iron Age settlements across the Altai region, the South-West Siberian Steppe and Forest-Steppe, and the North-West Siberian Taiga, Tundra, and Yugra, followed by medieval Ob-Ugrian, Selkup, Turkic, and Siberian Tatar fortified centres (*gorodki* and *gorodishcha*), and early modern to Imperial Russian towns, *ostrogi*, factory settlements (*zavody*), and *slobody*.

Prehistoric, Bronze Age, and Iron Age settlement populations are estimated from excavated house counts, dwelling floor areas ($4,5\text{ m}^2$ per person with a contemporaneity factor of $0,65$ to $0,8$), and geomagnetic settlement plans [@Piezonka2023; @Dubovtseva2020; @Borzunov2012; @Ryabogina2010; @Outram2009; @Epimakhov2009; @Krause2019; @Molodin2009; @Matveeva1999; @Losey2018; @Grushin2011; @Dashkovskiy2024]. Medieval Yugra and Siberian Khanate populations combine excavated *gorodishche* house counts with 17th-century Russian fur-tribute (*yasak*) registers using B. O. Dolgikh's household multipliers ($4$ per adult male payer for Ob-Ugrian and Selkup communities and $4,5$ to $5$ for Siberian Tatar *yurts*) [@Dolgikh1960; @Bakhrushin1935; @Kardash2009; @Vizgalov2008; @Vizgalov2011; @Solodkin2015; @Tataurov2016; @Maslyuzhenko2018; @Matveev2011; @Vodyasov2016; @Vodyasov2017; @Gorbunov2018]. Early modern and 18th- to 19th-century urban and *sloboda* populations are drawn from 17th-century garrison and household rosters (*dozornye* and *perepisnye knigi*), the ten Imperial Poll-Tax Revisions (1719-1858) [@Kabuzan1990; @Kolesnikov1973; @Rezun1989; @Korusenko2011], and the First All-Russian Imperial Census of 1897 [@Patkanov1911; @Tychinskikh2019; @Bryukhanova2021].
