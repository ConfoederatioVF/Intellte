# Abstract

Across the 122,9k km² of historical Circassia and Central Ciscaucasia,
urban and rural settlements have developed in four distinct waves from
the Eneolithic to 1900AD. Fortified Eneolithic and Early Bronze Age
villages at Meshoko, Svobodnoe and Galyugay, followed by Western
Caucasian dolmen settlements and Koban terrace villages around Mount
Elbrus and the Kislovodsk basin, preceded the classical urban network of
the Asiatic Bosporus and the Kuban plain, where Phanagoria (14k at
300BC), Gorgippia (8k at 1AD), Hermonassa and the Maeotian trading town
of Elizavetinskoye (5k at 300BC) anchored a dense grain-exporting
countryside
[\[1\]](#ref-korenevskiy2004)[\[2\]](#ref-kuznetsov2017phanagoria)[\[3\]](#ref-alekseeva1997anapa).

In the Middle Ages, the largest inland centres shifted to the Christian
Alanic cathedral towns and fortresses of the upper Kuban and Terek
valleys - led by Nizhniy Arkhyz (8,5k in 1000AD), Khumara and the two
Dzhulats (Verkhniy and Nizhniy Dzhulat) - alongside the Genoese-Zichian
ports of Matrega (Taman), Copa (Slavyansk) and Mapa (Anapa)
[\[4\]](#ref-kuznetsov1993)[\[5\]](#ref-khotko2015zichia). Following
Timur’s 1395 campaign, settlement dispersed into Circassian, Kabardian
and Abaza lowland and foothill villages (*kabaki*) and Ossetian and
Karachay-Balkar mountain stone *auls* (such as Dargavs and Kart-Dzhurt).
In the late 18th and 19th centuries, the founding of Russian fortresses,
the 1860-1864 *muhajir* expulsion and railway colonisation created a new
urban hierarchy led by Yekaterinodar (65,6k in 1897AD), Vladikavkaz
(43,7k), Yeysk (35,4k), Maykop (34,3k), Pyatigorsk (18,4k) and
Novorossiysk (16,9k)
[\[6\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905kuban)[\[7\]](#ref-kabuzan1996).

# Methodology

This gazetteer gives population estimates for 204 towns and villages
across Krasnodar Krai, Adygea, Karachay-Cherkessia, Kabardino-Balkaria,
North Ossetia and the Kislovodsk basin from 10000BC to 1900AD.

Prehistoric, Greek and Alanic figures are based on the size of excavated
settlements and cemeteries
[\[1\]](#ref-korenevskiy2004)[\[2\]](#ref-kuznetsov2017phanagoria)[\[3\]](#ref-alekseeva1997anapa)[\[4\]](#ref-kuznetsov1993)[\[8\]](#ref-formozov1965stone)[\[9\]](#ref-markovin1978dolmens)[\[10\]](#ref-reinhold2017)[\[11\]](#ref-belinskiy2018)[\[12\]](#ref-kuznetsov1992)[\[13\]](#ref-korobov2017).
Early modern and 19th-century estimates come from Genoese and Ottoman
port records, Russian embassy reports and records of the 1859-1864
expulsions
[\[5\]](#ref-khotko2015zichia)[\[14\]](#ref-kusheva1963)[\[15\]](#ref-gardanov1967social)[\[16\]](#ref-volkova1974)[\[17\]](#ref-khotko2001)[\[18\]](#ref-berger1860expulsion)[\[19\]](#ref-kumykov1994),
while the 1897 and 1900 figures are taken from the first Russian
imperial census
[\[6\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905kuban)[\[7\]](#ref-kabuzan1996)[\[20\]](#ref-bgeu2007demography)[\[21\]](#ref-golovlev2017census).
