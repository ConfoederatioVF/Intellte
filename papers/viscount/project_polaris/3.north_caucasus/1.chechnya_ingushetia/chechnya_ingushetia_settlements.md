# Abstract

Across the 20,8k km² of Chechnya and Ingushetia, settlement has shifted
repeatedly between the fertile Sunzha-Assa-Argun piedmont plain and the
defensive limestone canyons of the Greater Caucasus. Early Bronze Age
villages at Lugovoye and Bamut and Eastern Koban craft hillforts at
Serzhen-Yurt and Zandak (housing 400-800 residents apiece by 1000-500BC)
gave way in the first millennium AD to the walled Alanic towns of the
Sunzha and Assa plains, led by Alkhan-Kala (12k in 1200AD) and the Magas
settlement cluster around Nazran and Ekazhevo (18k in 1200AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-munchaev1961lugovoye)[\[2\]](#ref-kozenkova1992serzhen)[\[3\]](#ref-vinogradov1970)[\[4\]](#ref-chakhkiev2006magas).

The Mongol siege of Magas in 1239-1240 and Timur’s campaign of 1395
destroyed the lowland towns and drove the surviving population into the
fortified stone tower villages (*aul-kreposti*) of the Armkhi, Assa and
Argun gorges, including Egikal, Erzi, Targim, Vovnushki, Tsoy-Pede and
Khoy
[\[5\]](#ref-markovin1965monuments)[\[6\]](#ref-dolgieva2013ingushetia).
Between the 16th and 18th centuries, Vainakh settlers returned to the
foothills and plains to found large agricultural *auls* of 1k-4k
inhabitants at Shali, Urus-Martan, Aldy, Germenchuk, Chechen-Aul and
Nazran alongside the Greben and Terek Cossack *stanitsas* on the left
bank of the Terek
[\[7\]](#ref-kusheva1963peoples)[\[8\]](#ref-akhmadov2002chechnya)[\[9\]](#ref-kabuzan1996).
By 1897-1900AD, following the Caucasian War and the 1893 discovery of
the Grozny oilfield, Grozny had grown into an industrial city of
15,6k-18,5k inhabitants, flanked by major lowland villages and
Sunzha-Terek Cossack settlements
[\[10\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905terek)[\[11\]](#ref-ibragimova2006).

# Methodology

This gazetteer traces the population histories of 165 settlements across
Chechnya and Ingushetia from 10000BC to 1900AD.

Prehistoric, Eastern Koban and Alanic figures are derived from excavated
settlement areas and cemetery surveys
[\[1\]](#ref-munchaev1961lugovoye)[\[2\]](#ref-kozenkova1992serzhen)[\[3\]](#ref-vinogradov1970)[\[4\]](#ref-chakhkiev2006magas)[\[12\]](#ref-mamaev2009).
Highland stone tower and early modern estimates draw on architectural
surveys of the Assa, Armkhi and Argun gorges alongside Russian
diplomatic registers
[\[5\]](#ref-markovin1965monuments)[\[7\]](#ref-kusheva1963peoples)[\[8\]](#ref-akhmadov2002chechnya)[\[13\]](#ref-chakhkiev2007weapons)[\[14\]](#ref-akhmadov2019chechnya),
while 19th-century and 1897 totals follow military returns and the First
All-Russian Imperial Census for Terek Oblast
[\[9\]](#ref-kabuzan1996)[\[10\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905terek)[\[11\]](#ref-ibragimova2006)[\[15\]](#ref-berger1859chechnya).
