# Abstract

Across the 50,3k km² of Dagestan, urban and rural settlement has long
rested on a balance between the walled trading cities of the Caspian
coastal corridor and the terraced stone *auls* of the interior limestone
plateaus. Early Neolithic and Eneolithic mountain villages at Chokh,
Ginchi and Chinna and the Early Bronze Age Kura-Araxes mound complex at
Velikent (1,8k in 3000BC) were succeeded in classical antiquity and the
early Middle Ages by the Sassanian fortress city of Derbent (peaking at
40k in 1000AD) and the Khazar and Savir walled towns of Balanjar (15k in
700AD), Samandar/Tarki (12k in 800AD), Varachan/Urtseki (8k in 600AD)
and Endirey
[\[1\]](#ref-amirkhanov1987chokh)[\[2\]](#ref-gadzhiev1997velikent)[\[3\]](#ref-kudryavtsev1982derbent)[\[4\]](#ref-magomedov1983khazar).

While the Mongol and Timurid campaigns of the 13th and 14th centuries
devastated the lowland towns and reduced Derbent to 6k inhabitants by
1400AD, the fortified highland capitals and craft centres - Khunzakh,
Kumukh, Akusha, Chokh, Sogratl, Kubachi and Akhty - preserved a dense
mountain population of 2k-6,5k residents per major *aul* into the early
modern era
[\[5\]](#ref-umakhanov1973mutual)[\[6\]](#ref-aliev1975feudal)[\[7\]](#ref-aglarov1988jamaat).
After the Russian creation of Dagestan Oblast in 1860 and the arrival of
the Vladikavkaz Railway in the 1890s, coastal and lowland administrative
towns expanded rapidly alongside the mountain centres, led in
1897-1900AD by Derbent (14,6k-15,5k), Petrovsk-Port/Makhachkala
(9,8k-11k), Temir-Khan-Shura/Buynaksk (9,2k-9,5k), Kizlyar (7,3k-7,5k)
and Khasavyurt (5,3k-5,6k)
[\[8\]](#ref-kozubsky1901)[\[9\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905dagestan).

# Methodology

This gazetteer gives population estimates for 214 towns and villages
across Dagestan from 10000BC to 1900AD.

Prehistoric, Bronze Age and classical figures are based on the area of
excavated settlements and house counts
[\[1\]](#ref-amirkhanov1987chokh)[\[2\]](#ref-gadzhiev1997velikent)[\[3\]](#ref-kudryavtsev1982derbent)[\[4\]](#ref-magomedov1983khazar)[\[10\]](#ref-gadzhiev1991early)[\[11\]](#ref-abakarov1993atlas).
Medieval and early modern estimates come from local chronicles, Russian
embassy reports and village household lists
[\[5\]](#ref-umakhanov1973mutual)[\[6\]](#ref-aliev1975feudal)[\[7\]](#ref-aglarov1988jamaat)[\[12\]](#ref-minorsky1958bab)[\[13\]](#ref-aliev1999),
while 19th-century and 1897 totals are taken from the 1886 family lists
(*posemeynye spiski*) and the first Russian imperial census
[\[8\]](#ref-kozubsky1901)[\[9\]](#ref-troynitskiy1905dagestan)[\[14\]](#ref-kozubsky1895)[\[15\]](#ref-kabuzan1996).
