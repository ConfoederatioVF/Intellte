# Abstract

This gazetteer compiles coordinates, historical names and benchmark
population estimates for 191 settlements and archaeological sites across
the Wild Fields and Budjak (~238k km²) between the Lower Danube and the
Seversky Donets from 10000BC to 1400AD. Prehistoric entries cover Ice
Age and Mesolithic river camps (Anetovka II, Mirnoe and the Vasylivka
rapids cemeteries), Copper Age Bolgrad-Aldeni and Usatovo villages, the
Yamnaya stone fort at Mikhailovka and Late Bronze Age Sabatinovka
farming villages and Bakhmut copper mines (Kartamysh). Classical and
Late Antique entries track the Milesian Greek ports of Pontic Olbia
(12k - 15k in 500BC), Tyras (6k), Nikonion and Berezan, the 1,2k ha
Scythian ironworking town of Kamenskoye Gorodishche (6k), the 16 Late
Scythian stone forts on the Lower Dnieper and the dense village network
of the Chernyakhov culture up to 375AD. Medieval entries follow the
*Dridu* villages of Budjak, the Khazar-era Saltovo-Mayaki forts on the
Seversky Donets (led by Sidorovo at 5k in 900AD), the Kievan Rus river
ports of Oleshye (3,5k in 1200AD), Protolche and Igren-Samar, the
Polovtsian winter towns of Sharukhan, Sugrov and Balin, and the
fourteenth-century Golden Horde, Genoese, Moldavian and Lithuanian ports
of Aq Qerman (15k - 16k in 1300AD), Kiliia (6,5k - 8k), Velyki Kuchugury
(5,5k - 6k), Dashov and Khadzhibey.

# Methodology

The 191 settlements and archaeological sites of the Wild Fields and
Budjak (~238k km²) recorded between 10000BC and 1400AD are listed below
across its five regions: Budjak (I), the north-western Pontic steppe
(II), the right-bank Bug-Dnieper and Ingul-Ingulets uplands (III), the
left-bank Lower Dnieper, Veliky Lug and northern Taurida steppe (IV) and
the Middle Seversky Donets and Azov basin (V).

Each entry gives the settlement name, historical or alternative names,
coordinates, dates of occupation and population estimates. Figures are
based on site area and house counts for prehistoric camps and Bronze Age
villages, Greek town and rural hinterland surveys, *Dridu* and
*Saltovo-Mayaki* fort enclosures, Kievan Rus chronicle and excavation
evidence and Golden Horde, Genoese, Moldavian and Lithuanian port
records up to 1400AD
