# Abstract

This gazetteer compiles location coordinates, alternative Greek,
Scythian, Byzantine and Tatar names and benchmark population estimates
for 171 settlements and archaeological sites across the Crimean
Peninsula (~26,95k km²) from 10000BC to 1400AD. In prehistory and the
Bronze Age, settlement moved from the Mesolithic limestone rock-shelters
of the Inner Ridge (Shan-Koba, Murzak-Koba and Fatma-Koba) to Kemi-Oba
valley settlements and the fortified Tauri promontory of Uch-Bash
(0,9k - 1,2k in 1000BC). After 600BC, Milesian and Dorian colonists
built Panticapaeum (28k in 200AD), Chersonesus Taurica (20k), Theodosia
and Kerkinitis alongside the Late Scythian capital at Scythian Neapolis
(8k) and the Roman fort at Charax, before Gothic and Hunnic raids
emptied the open countryside in the third and fourth centuries AD.
Medieval town life revived under Byzantine and Khazar rule at Cherson
(12k in 900AD), Sugdaia (9k) and the cliff-top plateau towns of
Mangup-Doros (7k) and Eski-Kermen (5,2k), reaching a fourteenth-century
commercial peak at Genoese Caffa (32k - 40k in 1300 - 1345AD) and Golden
Horde Solkhat (28k) before the Black Death and the raids of Timur and
Edigu reduced urban numbers by 1400AD.

# Methodology

The 171 towns, villages and archaeological sites of Crimea (~26,95k km²)
recorded between 10000BC and 1400AD are listed below with their
historical or alternative names, coordinates, dates of occupation and
estimated populations.

For the Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic and Bronze Age (10000BC -
1000BC), figures are based on the floor area of rock-shelters and open
camps
[\[1\]](#ref-cherkasov2018)[\[2\]](#ref-shchepinsky1966)[\[3\]](#ref-kris1981).
For Classical Chersonesus, the Bosporan Kingdom and Late Scythia
(500BC - 400AD), estimates come from walled town areas and surveys of
the surrounding farmland
[\[4\]](#ref-maslennikov1998)[\[5\]](#ref-carter2000)[\[6\]](#ref-popova2011).
For the Middle Ages (500AD - 1400AD) - covering Byzantine Cherson,
Crimean Gothia, Khazar settlements, Kievan Rus Korsun and Korchev, the
Principality of Theodoro, Golden Horde Solkhat and the Genoese ports -
estimates are drawn from fortress walls, mountain plateau settlements
and town quarters
[\[7\]](#ref-aibabin1999)[\[8\]](#ref-baranov1990)[\[9\]](#ref-bocharov2017)[\[10\]](#ref-myts2024)[\[11\]](#ref-ozturk2019)[\[12\]](#ref-inalcik1996)[\[13\]](#ref-vodarsky2003).
