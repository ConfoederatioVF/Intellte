# Abstract

This gazetteer compiles population estimates for 185 excavated
settlements across the 176,2k km² territory of Galicia, Volhynia,
Podolia, Bukovina and Transcarpathia from the Upper Palaeolithic
(10000BC) to the Kingdom of Rus (1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-krypyakevych1984galicia)[\[2\]](#ref-kotlyar1998galician)[\[3\]](#ref-kuza1989malye).
The prehistoric, Scythian and Roman-era series (Table ) trace 36 major
sites from the Dniester canyon mammoth camps of Molodova V and Korman IV
(110 - 180 inhabitants in 10000BC) through the Trypillia-Cucuteni
promontory settlements of Stina (3k at 3500BC), Bilche-Zolote (2,5k) and
Koshylivtsi (2,5k) to the 110 ha Scythian hillfort at Nemyriv (4,5k at
500BC) and the Chernyakhov craft centres of Lepesivka (1,5k at 300AD)
and Ripniv (1,4k)
[\[4\]](#ref-chernysh1987molodova)[\[5\]](#ref-videiko2004trypillia)[\[6\]](#ref-magomedov2001chernyakhov).

Across the 160 medieval towns and strongholds (Tables –), urban
populations reached their pre-Mongol height in 1200AD under the
Rostislavichi and Romanovichi dynasties, led by Halych (30k across 200
ha), Volodymyr (22k), Zvenyhorod (10k), Lutsk (10k), Peremyshl (9k),
Terebovlia (6,5k), Belz (6k) and Bakota (5,5k)
[\[7\]](#ref-tomenchuk2006halych)[\[8\]](#ref-petehyrych2006galicia)[\[9\]](#ref-tersky2006lutsk).
Following Batu Khan’s winter campaign of 1240-1241AD, which sacked
Halych (falling to 10k in 1250AD), Volodymyr (12k), Zvenyhorod (2,5k)
and Iziaslavl (600), urban life reorganized around the unstormed cliff
fortresses of Kremenets (4,5k in 1250AD, 5,5k in 1300AD) and Danyliv and
the new royal capitals founded by King Daniel Romanovich at Kholm (9k in
1250AD, 12k in 1300AD) and Lviv (4k in 1250AD, 12k in 1300AD).

# Methodology

Across the 176,2k km² territory of Galicia, Volhynia, Podolia, Bukovina
and Transcarpathia, archaeological excavations and chronicle records
document 185 principal settlements between the Upper Palaeolithic
(10000BC) and the post-Mongol Kingdom of Galicia-Volhynia (1250AD -
1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-krypyakevych1984galicia)[\[2\]](#ref-kotlyar1998galician)[\[10\]](#ref-vojtovych2011borders).

## Population Estimation from Archaeological Evidence

Population estimates for individual towns and villages draw on excavated
site areas, medieval rampart circuits and post-Mongol destruction
layers.

For the prehistoric, Eneolithic and Early Iron Age periods (10000BC -
300AD), Upper Palaeolithic and Neolithic figures (10000BC - 5000BC) are
based on the multilayered Dniester canyon and Tisza camps at Molodova I
and V, Korman IV, Babin I, Voronovitsa, Korolevo and Kulychivka (110 -
220 inhabitants) and the Bug-Dniester and Linear Pottery (*LBK*) farming
villages at Soroky, Bazkiv Ostriv and Nezvysko (280 - 420 inhabitants)
[\[4\]](#ref-chernysh1987molodova)[\[5\]](#ref-videiko2004trypillia).
Trypillia-Cucuteni estimates (3500BC) reflect the promontory and
concentric settlements of the Upper and Middle Dniester, the Horyn
flint-mining zone and the Southern Bug, including Stina (3k),
Bilche-Zolote (Verteba Cave, 2,5k), Koshylivtsi (2,5k), Zalishchyky
(2,2k), Nemyriv (2,2k), Bodaki (2k), Klishchiv (2k), Horodnytsia (1,8k)
and Shypyntsi (1,8k) [\[5\]](#ref-videiko2004trypillia). Scythian West
Podolian hillfort and Chernyakhov figures (500BC - 300AD) follow the 110
ha earthen rampart fort at Nemyriv (4,5k), Severynivka (2,5k) and
Hryhorivka (2,2k), together with the Roman-era Chernyakhov, Lipitsa and
Wielbark craft settlements at Lepesivka (1,5k), Ripniv II (1,4k),
Bovshiv (1,2k) and Cherneliv-Ruskyi (1,1k)
[\[6\]](#ref-magomedov2001chernyakhov)[\[11\]](#ref-smirnova1990nemyriv).

For Galician-Volhynian fortified towns (*gorodishcha*) and suburbs
(*posady*, 800AD - 1240AD), following the urban archaeological studies
of P. P. Tolochko
[\[12\]](#ref-tolochko1989goroda)[\[13\]](#ref-tolochko2001demography),
A. V. Kuza
[\[3\]](#ref-kuza1989malye)[\[14\]](#ref-kuza1996gorodishcha), V. M.
Petehyrych [\[8\]](#ref-petehyrych2006galicia), B. P. Tomenchuk
[\[7\]](#ref-tomenchuk2006halych) and S. V. Tersky
[\[9\]](#ref-tersky2006lutsk), towns are divided into the inner princely
citadel (*detinets*), outer rampart circuit (*okolnyi grad*) and
unfortified artisan quarters (*posady* and *pidhoroddia*). Compact
multi-rampart capitals such as Volodymyr (75 ha, 22k), Zvenyhorod (45
ha, 10k), Lutsk (40 ha, 10k), Peremyshl (38 ha, 9k), Kholm (36 ha, 8k in
1240AD), Terebovlia (30 ha, 6,5k), Belz (28 ha, 6k), Bakota (26 ha,
5,5k), Peresopnytsia (24 ha, 5k), Dorogobuzh-on-Horyn (22 ha, 4,5k),
Kamianets (22 ha, 4k) and Kremenets (20 ha, 4k) reflect mean urban
densities of 200 - 290 persons/ha across combined *detinets* and core
*posad* areas. Conversely, sprawling settlements enclosing meadows,
monasteries and river ports, notably Halych (*Krylos*, 200 ha, 30k, 150
persons/ha), Vasyliv (35 ha, 5k, 143 persons/ha) and the early tribal
forts of Plisnesk (160 ha) and Stilsko (200 ha), had lower overall
densities
[\[7\]](#ref-tomenchuk2006halych)[\[15\]](#ref-fylypchuk2012plisnesk).

For the Mongol conquest and the Kingdom of Rus (1240AD - 1300AD),
estimates reflect the 1240-1241AD destruction layers excavated at
Iziaslavl (falling from 2,8k in 1200AD to 600 in 1250AD)
[\[16\]](#ref-karger1964iziaslavl), Kolodyazhyn (2,2k to 450),
Zvenyhorod (10k to 2,5k), Halych (30k to 10k) and Volodymyr (22k to
12k), contrasted with the unconquered cliff citadels of Kremenets (4k in
1200AD to 4,5k in 1250AD and 5,5k in 1300AD) and Danyliv, and the new
royal capitals at Kholm (12k in 1300AD) and Lviv (12k in 1300AD)
[\[17\]](#ref-kotlyar2002chronicle)[\[18\]](#ref-rappoport1967military).
