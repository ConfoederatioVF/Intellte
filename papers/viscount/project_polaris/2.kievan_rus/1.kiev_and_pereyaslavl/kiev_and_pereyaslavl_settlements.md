# Abstract

This gazetteer presents population series for 145 excavated settlements
across the ~265k km² Middle and Lower Dnieper basin from the Upper
Palaeolithic (10000BC) to the Golden Horde appanage era (1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-tolochko1983)[\[2\]](#ref-kuza1996)[\[3\]](#ref-motsia2007).
The prehistoric and ancient tables cover 52 sites, moving from the
mammoth-bone camps of Mezhirich and Kirillovskaya (80 - 160 inhabitants
in 10000BC) to the Trypillia BII-CI concentric proto-towns of the
Cherkasy-Uman plateau at 3600BC (Talianky at 15k, Maidanetske at 10k and
Nebelivka at 9k) and the Scythian forest-steppe hillforts and Greek
colonies of 500BC (Pontic Olbia at 15k, Belsk-Gelonus at 12k, Kamenskoye
at 6k and Trakhtemyriv at 4,5k)
[\[4\]](#ref-videiko2004)[\[5\]](#ref-shramko1987)[\[6\]](#ref-kryzhitsky1999).

Across the 95 medieval Rus towns and frontier fortresses (1000AD -
1300AD), urban growth peaked in 1200AD under the protection of the
Stuhna, Irpin, Ros (Porossia) and Sula (Posullya) rampart lines. Kiev
rose from 28k in 1000AD to 50k in 1200AD across 380 ha, flanked by
Belgorod (9k), Vyshhorod (7k), Pereyaslavl-Russky (15k), the Chorni
Klobuky frontier capital of Torchesk (6k) and the Drevlian centres of
Ovruch (4,5k) and Iskorosten (4k). Batu Khan’s campaigns of 1239-1240AD
devastated the exposed forest-steppe forts and reduced Kiev to 4k in
1250AD (8,5k in 1300AD) and Pereyaslavl to 3,5k (4,5k in 1300AD),
whereas the wooded Drevlian towns retained half of their inhabitants and
the tribute-paying Bolokhov towns survived intact until Daniel of
Galicia’s punitive expedition of 1257AD
[\[7\]](#ref-ivakin1996)[\[8\]](#ref-morgunov2003)[\[9\]](#ref-tomashevsky2008).

# Methodology

Across the Middle and Lower Dnieper (~265k km²), archaeological
excavations and Rus chronicles document 145 principal settlements
between the Upper Palaeolithic (10000BC), the Mongol campaigns of
1239-1240 and the Golden Horde appanage era (1250AD - 1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-tolochko1983)[\[2\]](#ref-kuza1996)[\[3\]](#ref-motsia2007).

## Population Estimation from Archaeological Evidence

Population estimates for individual towns and villages draw on excavated
site areas, medieval rampart circuits and post-Mongol destruction
layers.

For prehistoric and Early Iron Age sites (10000BC - 500BC), Upper
Palaeolithic and Neolithic figures (10000BC - 6000BC) are based on the
mammoth-bone dwelling rings at Mezhirich, Dobranichevka, Gontsy and
Kirillovskaya (100 - 250 inhabitants) and the Dnieper-Donets cemeteries
at Nikolskoye and Vovnigi (300 - 450 inhabitants)
[\[4\]](#ref-videiko2004)[\[10\]](#ref-tolochko1972). Trypillia BII-CI
figures (3600BC) follow geomagnetic house counts across the
Cherkasy-Uman plateau between the Ros and the Southern Bug (Talianky at
450 ha with ~2,7k houses; Maidanetske at 200 ha with ~2,1k houses;
Nebelivka at 260 ha with ~1,45k houses; and Dobrovody at 250 ha),
assuming 75 - 80% of the houses were occupied at the same time by 5,5 -
6,5 persons per two-storey *ploshchadka* homestead
[\[4\]](#ref-videiko2004)[\[11\]](#ref-diachenko2012)[\[12\]](#ref-kruts1989).
Scythian forest-steppe hillfort and Greek colonial figures (500BC)
follow excavated housing quarters at Belsk (Gelonus, 4,02k ha perimeter,
152,6 ha across the Western, Eastern and Kuzeminsk forts, 12k),
Trakhtemyriv (500 ha, 4,5k), Motronyn (200 ha, 4k), Nemirov (110 ha, 4k)
and the 50 ha stone-walled town of Olbia (15k)
[\[5\]](#ref-shramko1987)[\[6\]](#ref-kryzhitsky1999)[\[13\]](#ref-daragan2017).

For Kievan Rus fortified towns (*gorodishcha*) and suburbs (*posady*,
800AD - 1240AD), following P. P. Tolochko
[\[1\]](#ref-tolochko1983)[\[14\]](#ref-tolochko1989), A. V. Kuza
[\[2\]](#ref-kuza1996)[\[15\]](#ref-kuza1989) and M. A. Sahaydak
[\[16\]](#ref-sahaydak1991), towns are divided into the inner citadel
(*detinets*), outer walled town (*okolny gorod*) and unfortified
riverside craft suburbs (*posady* and *podol*). Inside the densely built
*detinets* and *okolny gorod* ramparts, average homestead (*usadba*)
plots measure 300 - 500 m² (20 - 25 homesteads per hectare at 5,5 - 6
persons per household, or 110 - 150 persons/ha). In outer *posad*
quarters and the cavalry towns of the Porossia and Posullya frontier
lines, wider courtyard plots (600 - 900 m²) give 70 - 110 persons/ha
[\[3\]](#ref-motsia2007)[\[8\]](#ref-morgunov2003).

For the Mongol conquest and late thirteenth-century recovery (1240AD -
1300AD), estimates reflect the 1239-1240AD destruction layers across
Pereyaslavl, Posullya, Porossia and Kiev, John of Plano Carpini’s 1246AD
eyewitness report of ~200 surviving households in Kievan *Podol* (4k
inhabitants in Kiev in 1250AD, recovering to 8,5k by 1300AD according to
H. Yu. Ivakin) and the survival of the Drevlian Polesia and Bolokhov
Land towns prior to Daniel of Galicia’s 1257AD campaign
[\[7\]](#ref-ivakin1996)[\[9\]](#ref-tomashevsky2008)[\[17\]](#ref-dawson1955).
