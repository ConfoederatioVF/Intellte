# Abstract

This gazetteer compiles population estimates for 172 excavated
settlements across the 207,6k km² river basins of Polotsk, Turov and
Belarusian Rus from the Final Palaeolithic (10000BC) to the early Grand
Duchy of Lithuania (1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-shtykhov1992goroda)[\[2\]](#ref-zagorulsky2001arkheologiya)[\[3\]](#ref-lysenko2004drevniy).
The prehistoric and Iron Age series cover 24 representative sites,
including the mammoth- and reindeer-hunter camps of Yurevichi and
Berdizh (40 - 65 inhabitants in 10000BC), the waterlogged Neolithic pile
dwellings of the Krivina peat basin at Asavets (160 - 340 inhabitants in
2500BC), the Milograd, Zarubintsy and Brushed Pottery hillforts (320 -
560 inhabitants in 500BC - 200AD) and the tenth-century craft complex on
the Menka river (2,4k in 1000AD)
[\[4\]](#ref-charniauski1997arkhealogiya)[\[5\]](#ref-isaenko1999arkhealogiya)[\[6\]](#ref-zagorulsky1982vozniknovenie).

Across the 148 medieval fortified towns (*gorodishcha*) occupied between
800AD and 1300AD, urban populations reached their pre-Mongol peak in
1200AD under the Izyaslavichi princes of Polotsk and the Rurikid rulers
of Turov-Pinsk and Black Ruthenia, led by Polotsk (25k across 190 ha),
Turov (12k), Vitebsk (9,5k), Minsk (9k), Grodno (8,5k), Pinsk (8k),
Berestye (7,5k), Novogrudok (7,5k) and Gomel (6,5k)
[\[7\]](#ref-tarasov1998polotsk)[\[8\]](#ref-lysenko1985berestye)[\[9\]](#ref-gurevich1981drevniy).
While Batu Khan’s columns of 1239-1241AD sacked Gomel (falling to 3,5k
in 1250AD), Turov (7k), Mozyr (2,1k) and Berestye (5,2k) along the
southern frontier, the northern and western capitals of Polotsk (22k in
1250AD, 23k in 1300AD), Vitebsk (9,5k in 1300AD), Minsk (8,8k), Grodno
(8,5k) and Novogrudok (rising to 9k in 1300AD as Mindaugas’s early seat)
escaped destruction and anchored the early Lithuanian-Ruthenian realm.

# Results

## Prehistoric, Neolithic, Bronze Age and Iron Age Sites

For the 24 prehistoric and Iron Age settlements across the period
10000BC - 500AD, population estimates are based on excavated house
counts, settled areas and household sizes (5 - 7 persons per hearth or
sunken dwelling) established by M. M. Charniauski, U. F. Isaenko, A. G.
Mitrofanov and L. D. Pobol
[\[4\]](#ref-charniauski1997arkhealogiya)[\[5\]](#ref-isaenko1999arkhealogiya)[\[10\]](#ref-mitrofanov1978zhelezny)[\[11\]](#ref-pobol1983arkheologicheskie).
Among the Final Palaeolithic and Mesolithic camps (10000BC - 5000BC),
*Yurevichi* (51,94°N, 29,53°E) on the Pripyat, *Berdizh* (52,83°N,
30,93°E) and *Grensk* (52,97°N, 30,89°E) on the Sozh, *Korma*,
*Ankovichi* and *Borok* were reindeer- and mammoth-hunter camps of 40 -
65 people at 10000BC, growing to 70 - 110 inhabitants in Mesolithic
lakeside camps at 5000BC. During the Neolithic pile-dwelling and
flint-mining period (5000BC - 2500BC), the waterlogged pile-dwellings at
Asavets (*Krivina peat bog*, 54,96°N, 29,42°E), Kryvina 1 and the
Krasnoselsky flint-mining settlement (53,26°N, 24,43°E) housed
communities of 160 - 340 inhabitants.

In the Bronze Age and Iron Age hillfort era (1200BC - 500AD), the large
lowland forts of the Milograd and Zarubintsy cultures in southern
Belarus (*Milograd* with 480 in 500BC, *Chaplin* with 560 in 200AD,
*Goroshkov* with 490 in 200AD and *Mokhov* with 450 in 500AD) were
larger than the small cliff-top hillforts of the Brushed Pottery and
Dnieper-Dvina cultures further north (*Malyshki* with 350 in 200AD,
*Zazony* with 320 in 200AD and *Bancerovshchina* with 490 in 500AD). At
the junction of the Menka and Dunai streams 16 km west of modern Minsk,
the *Menka* settlement (a 1,5 ha ramparted hillfort and 40 ha open craft
quarter excavated by E. M. Zagorulsky) served as the early centre of
*Mensk* (850 in 800AD, 1,6k in 900AD and 2,4k in 1000AD) before the
1067AD Battle on the Nemiga River and the move of the fortress to the
Svisloch-Nemiga *Zamchishche* at Minsk, after which the old site shrank
to an open country village (950 in 1100AD, 500 in 1200AD and 420 in
1300AD) [\[6\]](#ref-zagorulsky1982vozniknovenie).

## Pre-Mongol Kievan Rus and Post-1240AD Principality Centres

For the 148 fortified medieval towns (*gorodishcha*) occupied between
800AD and 1300AD, town populations are estimated from excavated rampart
areas (*Detinets* or *Zamchishche* plus *Okolny Gorod*) and open craft
suburbs (*Posady*), following the urban studies of G. V. Shtykhov (1975,
1992), S. V. Tarasov (1998), P. F. Lysenko (1974, 1985, 2004), E. M.
Zagorulsky (1982, 2001), F. D. Gurevich (1981), Ya. G. Zverugo (1989)
and A. V. Kuza (1989, 1996), which reckon 110 - 150 persons/ha inside
the timber-street *detinets* and *okolny gorod* quarters and 60 - 90
persons/ha in the outer *posady*
