# Abstract

This gazetteer compiles population estimates for 142 excavated
settlements across the 72,24k km² Southern Core of the Chernigov and
Novgorod-Seversk lands (modern Chernihiv and Sumy provinces and the
lower Sozh corridor around Gomel) from the Upper Palaeolithic (10000BC)
to 1300AD
[\[1\]](#ref-kovalenko2008chernihiv)[\[2\]](#ref-morgunov2003posemye)[\[3\]](#ref-kuza1989malye).
Early entries trace the mammoth-hunter winter camps of the middle Desna
gorge (Mezin and Pushkari I, 75 - 120 inhabitants in 10000BC), the Early
Iron Age promontory forts of the Yukhnovo and Milograd cultures (380 -
780 inhabitants in 500BC) and the tenth-century Varangian-Rus retinue
bases at Shestovytsia (3,2k in 1000AD), Lyubech (3,5k) and Boldyni Hory
(2,5k).

Under the Olgovichi dynasty in 1200AD, the urban network reached 120
fortified towns (*gorodishcha*), headed by the 330 ha capital at
Chernigov (35k), the Seversk seats of Novgorod-Seversk (12k) and Putivl
(7,5k), and the river towns of Gomel (6,5k), Snowsk (5k), Kursk (8k in
the northern forest-steppe), Hlukhiv (4,5k) and Starodub (4,5k).
Following Möngke and Batu’s October 1239AD campaign across the Desna and
Seym valleys, 68% of the southern frontier forts were abandoned;
Chernigov fell to 4k in 1250AD (recovering to 6k by 1300AD), while
princely authority shifted into the wooded refuges of Hlukhiv (4,5k in
1300AD) and Bryansk.

# Methodology

## Overview of the Settlement Gazetteer

Across the 72,24k km² Southern Core of the Chernigov-Seversk lands
(Chernihivska Oblast, Sumska Oblast and the Eastern Gomel and Lower Sozh
corridor), archaeological surveys and Rus chronicles document 142 major
settlements between 10000BC and 1300AD. These range from the Upper
Palaeolithic mammoth-bone camps of the middle Desna gorge and the Early
Iron Age promontory forts of the Yukhnovo and Milograd cultures to
tenth-century Varangian-Rus retinue bases and the fortified
*gorodishcha* of the Olgovichi principalities prior to and after the
Mongol sack of October 1239. Each entry in the tables below gives the
settlement’s historical name, geographic coordinates (50,14°N - 53,13°N,
29,83°E - 35,26°E) and estimated population across the benchmark dates.

## Population Estimation from Archaeological Evidence

For the Upper Palaeolithic to Early Iron Age (10000BC - 500AD, 22
sites), mammoth-hunter winter camps on the middle Desna (Mezin, Pushkari
I, Chulatovo I-II, Novgorod-Seversky and Buzhany) are reckoned at 75 -
120 persons per camp
[\[4\]](#ref-shovkoplyas1965mezinskaya)[\[5\]](#ref-boriskovsky1953paleolit)[\[6\]](#ref-velichko1997mammoth).
Yukhnovo and Milograd Early Iron Age hillforts (0,5 - 3 ha) are
estimated at 380 - 780 inhabitants [\[7\]](#ref-tretyakov1966finno),
while Late Zarubintsy, Kiev culture (Roishche and Harivka-Desna) and
Kolochin (Kolochin I) riverside villages are estimated at 580 - 850
inhabitants
[\[8\]](#ref-byrnya1991ranneslavyanskie)[\[9\]](#ref-symonovich1983chernyakhov).

For the Volyntsevo, Romny-Severian and Viking-Age centres (700AD -
1000AD), major Severian and Radimichi hillforts (Volyntsevo, Bytytsia,
Gornal, Novotroitske and Romny) and Varangian-Rus garrison towns
(Shestovytsia at 3,2k, Boldyni Hory at 2,5k, Mokhov at 1,95k and Lyubech
at 3,5k) are estimated from excavated house counts (*poluzemlyanki*) and
barrow cemeteries
[\[10\]](#ref-sukhobokov1975slaviane)[\[11\]](#ref-motsia2011shestovytsia)[\[12\]](#ref-androschuk2013vikings).

For the Olgovichi principalities at their twelfth- and early
thirteenth-century peak (1050AD - 1239AD), walled *detinets* and *okolny
gorod* ramparts (120 - 150 inh/ha) and outer *posad* and *podol* craft
suburbs (75 - 100 inh/ha) follow the archaeological surveys of V. P.
Kovalenko [\[1\]](#ref-kovalenko2008chernihiv), A. V. Kuza
[\[3\]](#ref-kuza1989malye)[\[13\]](#ref-kuza1996gorodishcha), P. P.
Tolochko [\[14\]](#ref-tolochko1989goroda), A. K. Zaitsev
[\[15\]](#ref-zaitsev1975chernigov), Yu. Yu. Morgunov
[\[2\]](#ref-morgunov2003posemye) and H. V. Shtykhov
[\[16\]](#ref-shtykhov1992goroda). For the October 1239AD Mongol sack
and the post-conquest principalities (1250AD - 1300AD), estimates
reflect excavated destruction layers across the Posemye and Desna
valleys (Putivl, Vyr, Vorgol, Vsevolozh and Chernigov) and the survival
of Hlukhiv (4,5k in 1300AD), Novgorod-Seversk (4,5k), Putivl (3,8k),
Gomel (3,4k) and Chernigov (6k)
[\[17\]](#ref-dimnik2003olgovichi)[\[18\]](#ref-kuczynski1936ziemie).
