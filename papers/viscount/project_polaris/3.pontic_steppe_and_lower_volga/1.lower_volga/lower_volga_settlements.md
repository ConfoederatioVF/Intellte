# Abstract

Across the 245k km² of the Lower Volga, settlement swung repeatedly
between the open Kalmyk and Trans-Volga steppes and the river corridor
of the Volga Delta, the Volga-Akhtuba Floodplain and the
Saratov-Volgograd valley. From Mesolithic and Neolithic fishing camps at
Seroglazovka, Kairshak, Varfolomeyevka (60-100 inhabitants in 5500BC)
and Dzhangar
[\[1\]](#ref-koltsov2005mezolit)[\[2\]](#ref-yudin2004varfolomeyevka)[\[3\]](#ref-baratskov2015itogi)
and Copper Age cemeteries at Khvalynsk I-II
[\[4\]](#ref-agapov1990khvalynskiy), permanent villages spread across
the northern river valleys during the Late Bronze Age Srubnaya period
(1500BC) [\[5\]](#ref-malov2008nizhneye)[\[6\]](#ref-lopatin2014nachalo)
before giving way to Sauromatian and Sarmatian nomadic centres such as
Kosika and Verkhnoye Pogromnoye [\[7\]](#ref-skripkin2012chislennosti).
In the Middle Ages, the delta and floodplain hosted the largest cities
on the Lower Volga: Khazar Itil at Semibugry/Barany Bugor (30k in 900AD)
and Samosdelka/Saksin (20k in 1200AD)
[\[8\]](#ref-vasilyev2015saksin)[\[9\]](#ref-vasilyev2020samosdelka)[\[10\]](#ref-solovyev2022semibugry),
followed under the Golden Horde (1340AD) by Sarai al-Jadid at
Tsarevskoye (85k), Sarai Batu at Selitrennoye (68k), Hajji-Tarkhan
(22k), Ukek with its 53 rural villages (9,5k urban and 15k rural) and
Beldzhamen (7,5k)
[\[11\]](#ref-yegorov1985geografiya)[\[12\]](#ref-nedashkovsky2010goroda)[\[13\]](#ref-pigarev2021topografii)[\[14\]](#ref-shigapov2022selishch).
After Timur sacked the floodplain capitals in 1395-1396AD, Russian
garrison towns founded at Astrakhan (1558), Tsaritsyn (1589), Saratov
(1590) and Kamyshin (1668), together with the 104 Volga German colonies
established in 1764-1767 and the Inner (Bukey) Kazakh centre of
Khanskaya Stavka (1801), grew into a dense network of river ports and
farming towns led in 1897 by Saratov (137,1k), Astrakhan (112,9k),
Tsaritsyn (55,2k) and Volsk (27,1k)
[\[15\]](#ref-pleve1998nemetskiye)[\[16\]](#ref-doenninghaus2011nemetskikh)[\[17\]](#ref-belousov2019bukeyevskoy)[\[18\]](#ref-troynitsky1904saratov)[\[19\]](#ref-troynitsky1904astrakhan).

# Methodology

This gazetteer compiles 146 camps, villages, towns and farming colonies
across the Lower Volga from 10000BC to 1900AD, giving historical and
alternative names, geographic coordinates and population estimates at
benchmark dates.

Population figures are derived from excavated pit-house floor areas and
cemetery cohorts for Mesolithic, Neolithic, Copper Age, Bronze Age
Srubnaya and Sarmatian sites
[\[1\]](#ref-koltsov2005mezolit)[\[2\]](#ref-yudin2004varfolomeyevka)[\[3\]](#ref-baratskov2015itogi)[\[4\]](#ref-agapov1990khvalynskiy)[\[5\]](#ref-malov2008nizhneye)[\[6\]](#ref-lopatin2014nachalo)[\[7\]](#ref-skripkin2012chislennosti)[\[20\]](#ref-smagin2018mikrolitov)[\[21\]](#ref-vybornov2022khronologiya)[\[22\]](#ref-tsibin2019maksimovka)[\[23\]](#ref-balabanova2009khronologicheskiye),
urban and rural settlement excavations for Khazar Itil, Pre-Mongol
Saksin and Golden Horde cities
[\[8\]](#ref-vasilyev2015saksin)[\[9\]](#ref-vasilyev2020samosdelka)[\[10\]](#ref-solovyev2022semibugry)[\[11\]](#ref-yegorov1985geografiya)[\[12\]](#ref-nedashkovsky2010goroda)[\[13\]](#ref-pigarev2021topografii)[\[14\]](#ref-shigapov2022selishch)[\[24\]](#ref-zilivinskaya2014ocherki)[\[25\]](#ref-fyodorovdavydov1994goroda)[\[26\]](#ref-nedashkovsky2000ukek)[\[27\]](#ref-nedashkovsky2022predmety)[\[28\]](#ref-zeleneev2018selitrennom)
and Russian garrison rolls, Imperial tax revisions (*revizii*), Volga
German colony censuses, Kalmyk and Inner (Bukey) Kazakh Horde records
and the 1897 Census
[\[15\]](#ref-pleve1998nemetskiye)[\[16\]](#ref-doenninghaus2011nemetskikh)[\[17\]](#ref-belousov2019bukeyevskoy)[\[18\]](#ref-troynitsky1904saratov)[\[19\]](#ref-troynitsky1904astrakhan)[\[29\]](#ref-zaitsev2006astrakhanskoye)[\[30\]](#ref-syzranov2014astrakhani)[\[31\]](#ref-trepavlov2016nogayskoy)[\[32\]](#ref-moiseev2015stepnyye)[\[33\]](#ref-kabuzan1963narodonaseleniye)[\[34\]](#ref-solovyov2013saratovskoy)[\[35\]](#ref-brylyov2018volgogradskoy)[\[36\]](#ref-ochirov2008tyurkoyazychnyye)[\[37\]](#ref-okonova2012migratsii)[\[38\]](#ref-kyshpanakov2026kalmyki)[\[39\]](#ref-pirova2020tatarakh).
