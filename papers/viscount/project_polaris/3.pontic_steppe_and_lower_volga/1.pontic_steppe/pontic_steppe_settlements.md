# Abstract

Across the 165k km² of the Russian Pontic Steppe, towns and villages
clustered along the Lower Don, the Azov coast and the Taman Peninsula
while the inland Sal-Manych and Don-Donets watersheds shifted from
nomadic grazing lands to Cossack and peasant farming districts after
1700AD. Early riverine fishing villages at Rakushechny Yar (50-120
residents in 6000-5000BC)
[\[1\]](#ref-belanovskaya1995rakushechnyy)[\[2\]](#ref-tsybrij2018rakushechnyy)
and Late Bronze Age Srubnaya and Kobyakovo settlements
[\[3\]](#ref-sharafutdinova1980kobyakovo)[\[4\]](#ref-potapov2013krest)
were followed in the Classical and Roman eras by the Bosporan Greek
cities of Phanagoria (12,5k in 350BC), Gorgippia (7,5k), Hermonassa
(5,5k) and Kepoi (3,8k) on Taman
[\[5\]](#ref-paromov1992karta)[\[6\]](#ref-kuznetsov2010phanagoria)[\[7\]](#ref-garbuzov2013selskaya)
and the delta emporia of Elizavetovskoye (3,8k) and Tanais (4k in 150AD)
with its ten Maeotian forts
[\[8\]](#ref-marchenko2000elizavetovskoye)[\[9\]](#ref-vdovchenkov2019meotov).
In the Middle Ages, the Khazar fortresses of Sarkel (3,2k in 900AD),
Semikarakorskoye (2,8k) and Tamatarkha (10k) anchored the Saltovo-Mayaki
village network
[\[10\]](#ref-artamonov1958sarkel)[\[11\]](#ref-afanasyev1993donskiye),
and under the Golden Horde the port of Azak/Tana reached 54k inhabitants
in 1340AD before falling to 13k on the eve of Timur’s 1395 sack
[\[12\]](#ref-maslovsky2024chislennosti). From the 16th century, Don
Cossack island *gorodki* at Razdory and Cherkassk (7,5k in 1700AD) and
18th- and 19th-century port, railway and coal towns transformed the
region, led in 1897 by Rostov-on-Don and Nakhichevan-on-Don (147,9k
combined), Novocherkassk (52k), Taganrog (51,4k), Yeysk (35,4k) and Azov
(27,5k)
[\[13\]](#ref-mininkov1998donskoye)[\[14\]](#ref-troynitsky1905don)[\[15\]](#ref-troynitsky1905kuban)[\[16\]](#ref-volvenko2023priazovya).

# Methodology

This gazetteer compiles 217 settlements across the Russian Pontic Steppe
from 10000BC to 1900AD, recording historical and alternative names,
geographic coordinates and benchmark population estimates.

Population figures are derived from excavated dwelling floor areas and
settlement plans for prehistoric and Bronze Age sites
[\[1\]](#ref-belanovskaya1995rakushechnyy)[\[2\]](#ref-tsybrij2018rakushechnyy)[\[3\]](#ref-sharafutdinova1980kobyakovo)[\[4\]](#ref-potapov2013krest)[\[17\]](#ref-dolbunova2021rakovinnyye)[\[18\]](#ref-skorobogatov2025keramicheskiy),
walled urban areas and rural surveys for Classical Bosporan, Maeotian
and Sarmatian settlements
[\[5\]](#ref-paromov1992karta)[\[6\]](#ref-kuznetsov2010phanagoria)[\[7\]](#ref-garbuzov2013selskaya)[\[8\]](#ref-marchenko2000elizavetovskoye)[\[9\]](#ref-vdovchenkov2019meotov)[\[19\]](#ref-garbuzov2011osvoyeniye)[\[20\]](#ref-kopylov2016vremeni)[\[21\]](#ref-batieva2011naseleniye),
fortress and urban excavations for Khazar Saltovo-Mayaki and Golden
Horde cities
[\[10\]](#ref-artamonov1958sarkel)[\[11\]](#ref-afanasyev1993donskiye)[\[12\]](#ref-maslovsky2024chislennosti)[\[22\]](#ref-flyorov2011goroda)[\[23\]](#ref-maslovsky2017azak)[\[24\]](#ref-maslovsky2022tokhtamysh)
and Russian *Posolsky Prikaz* rolls, Imperial tax revisions (*revizii*)
and the 1897 Census for Don and Kuban Cossack *stanitsas*, peasant
*slobodas* and cities
[\[13\]](#ref-mininkov1998donskoye)[\[14\]](#ref-troynitsky1905don)[\[15\]](#ref-troynitsky1905kuban)[\[16\]](#ref-volvenko2023priazovya)[\[25\]](#ref-sushchiy2019institut)[\[26\]](#ref-prudnikov2024gorodok)[\[27\]](#ref-pronshtein1961zemlya)[\[28\]](#ref-kabuzan1976zaseleniye)[\[29\]](#ref-agafonov2017zemli)[\[30\]](#ref-kolomeytseva2018krestyanstva)[\[31\]](#ref-burykina2008osvoyeniye)[\[32\]](#ref-parshina2023kubanskogo).
